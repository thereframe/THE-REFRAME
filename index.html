Foundational Science. Radical Ownership.

Most high performers are not broken. They are under-supported.

The standard model of health is linear: identify a symptom, prescribe a fix, send you back to the line. It treats your body as a machine with faulty parts, and your behaviour as a discipline problem. Neither is true — and for people operating under sustained load, that model fails quietly and repeatedly, because it was never designed for interconnected systems.

Your biology and your behaviour are one system. Cortisol shapes decision-making. The microbiome affects mood and clarity. Identity structures decide which habits survive a demanding week. The Reframe is a whole-system performance practice: we integrate executive coaching and biomedical nutrition because the most powerful shifts in thinking, energy, resilience and output happen when the two are supported together — not separated.

Biological Intelligence. Behavioural Depth.

We listen before we speak

In high-stakes leadership you are constantly expected to broadcast: to direct, decide, deliver and perform. Very few spaces are built to listen to you. Listening at The Reframe is not passive silence — it is an active, double-layered diagnostic. Two specialists. Two layers. One goal: to support you.

We listen to your biology

Precise biomarker diagnostics read what your cellular chemistry, gut microbiome and nervous system are communicating under sustained strain. Your body is not failing itself — it is speaking logically, and the data lets us hear it exactly.

We listen to your behaviour

A quiet, unhurried, structured space to examine the identity structures, internal habits and unexamined friction that drive exhaustion — without shaming, without hand-holding, without asking you to perform for us.

Then, together, we close the loop.

Listening is where every engagement starts — never where it ends. What is heard becomes data, and data becomes a protocol you can act on this week. You should never leave a session feeling merely understood. You should leave it understood and equipped.

The work

One system. Two intelligences.

Behavioural architecture

Behavioural work begins with an assessment, followed by unhurried, confidential sessions examining the patterns underneath your performance: the identity you built to succeed, the internal rules you have never questioned, the friction you have normalised as the cost of leadership.

Then we make it structural. Together we design behavioural architecture by incorporating specific, concrete adjustments to how you decide, delegate, recover and communicate. Every session ends with something you can act on before the next one.

You will not be given affirmations. You will be given a clearer view of your own operating system, and the tools to redesign it.

Functional nutrition

Biological work begins with an assessment, followed by functional testing chosen in line with your symptoms: blood chemistry, methylation and mineral status, gut microbiome, hormonal and adrenal markers. We are not guessing at what your body needs, we are reading it.

From your data, we build a targeted nutrition protocol: specific nutrients, at specific levels, for your specific chemistry, alongside practical adjustments to how you eat, sleep and recover. Nothing generic, nothing restrictive for its own sake, and nothing you cannot sustain inside a full working life.

Optional retesting at defined intervals shows you in your own numbers that the system is rebuilding.

Tash's story

My life runs well. I have work I love, a family I adore, and a level of steadiness and energy I never thought would be possible.

But it wasn't always this way.

For most of my life, I operated below my potential. I was capable, driven and high-functioning on the surface, but underneath, I was constantly tired, unfocused and never quite at my best.

In my thirties, that imbalance caught up with me. Getting through a working day started to require enormous effort. I could perform, but at a cost. Every day I would push through, then collapse at home.

I saw countless doctors, tried different medications, invested heavily in mindset work, and spent thousands of pounds searching for a magic pill that would fix everything. I didn't find it.

At this point, coaching entered my life. I was able to use the powerful conversations I had in this space to stabilise, stop fighting myself, understand my limits and make better decisions about how to spend the energy I had. It didn't help me "release my potential" and become a billion-dollar businesswoman. It gave me something better: it enabled me to quietly hold myself together and just do life.

I then found the right specialist clinic to work with. For the first time, the picture made sense. My challenges weren't just psychological, they were biochemical and genetic.

From there, things began to change. It wasn't instant or linear. Healing came in phases, with progress and setbacks, and a lot of learning around how my beliefs, my diet, my sleep, the way I connected with people and the routines I created for myself directly affected my energy and my performance.

Throughout this, coaching remained the constant thread, helping me integrate what I was learning, stay objective during setbacks, and build consistency rather than swinging between extremes.

So today, my definition of success is more authentic and meaningful to me. It's about being able to live and perform at my best with what I have available to me.

My work now exists because of this journey. I help high-performing people who feel overloaded and exhausted get better at doing life, by combining coaching, nutrition and measurable data, including targeted biomedical testing, to really get to the heart of what is keeping them stuck and feeling like their performance doesn't match their ambition.

Lilly's story

I used to think exhaustion was just the price of loving your work.

I spent years in fashion believing that pushing harder was the answer to everything. The industry was fast, creative and exciting, and I gave it everything. On the surface, I was keeping up: hitting deadlines, travelling, always switched on. Underneath, I was running on empty.

It took my health quietly unravelling to show me that pushing harder was not the answer.

I was not looking after myself. Meals were an afterthought, sleep was whatever was left over, and stress had become so normal that I stopped noticing it. I felt stuck, not just in my career but in myself, and my health was quietly paying the price. I was tired in a way that rest didn't fix, and I knew something had to change, even if I didn't yet know what.

Looking for answers, I started to learn how food, sleep, stress and the body's chemistry are all connected. The more I understood, the more my own experience began to make sense. It was never a lack of willpower. My body was under-supported, and it had been telling me so for a long time.

That curiosity became a new path. I left fashion and completed my degree in nutritional therapy, then went on to complete advanced training in the Walsh approach to biomedical nutrition. This work looks at each person's individual biochemistry, including methylation, nutrient balance and genetics, and at how it shapes energy, mood, focus and resilience.

Seeing measurable data change the way people understood themselves changed everything for me. Clients who had been told "everything looks normal" could finally see why they felt the way they did, and what to do about it.

Today, working as a nutritional therapist, I use targeted biomedical testing to find what's really going on beneath the surface, and I build personalised plans that fit around real, busy lives. Change doesn't happen overnight. It comes in steps, with patience and consistency, and often with a few setbacks along the way.

My work exists because of this journey. I help high-performing people who feel depleted and stuck understand their own biology, so they can stop pushing against their bodies and start working with them. Together with Tash's coaching, we help people close the gap between how they want to live and perform, and what their body currently has capacity for.

How an engagement begins

The Complete Reframe Approach

Seven stages, one integrated plan, built around your data, your patterns and your calendar.

01

Discovery session

A 60–90 minute 1:1 with Tash exploring your current challenges, thinking patterns and stress responses.

02

Functional health assessment

A meticulous intake with Lilly covering symptoms, energy, sleep, digestion and stress: a granular read on how your core systems are functioning day to day. Functional testing is then pinpointed and prioritised around your body and your symptoms.

03

Joint investigation

Where The Reframe is unique. Tash and Lilly combine their expertise to pinpoint where the thinking–behaviour–biochemistry cycle is breaking down, and which is driving which.

04

Diagnostic testing

Including, but not limited to:

Blood panels

Nutrient levels, inflammation, blood sugar regulation, thyroid and iron status

Walsh biochemistry

Methylation, copper and zinc balance, histamine and pyrroles: the markers behind energy, mood and focus

Methylation profile

How well the body produces and uses its key methyl donors (SAM, SAH, homocysteine)

Hormones

Dried urine, saliva and blood panels mapping stress hormones, sex hormones and daily cortisol rhythm

Nutrient status

Magnesium, B vitamins, zinc, vitamin D, omega-3 and more

Metabolomix

Oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, methylation, clearance pathways and nutrient needs

Gut health

Digestion, microbiome balance and inflammation

Lifecode Gx

Inherited tendencies across methylation, neurotransmitters, hormones and nutrient needs, addressed through personalised nutrition and lifestyle change

05

Your bespoke programme

One integrated plan: coaching that shifts the fixed views driving stress, alongside targeted nutrition and lifestyle interventions drawn from your results.

06

Ongoing delivery

Coaching and nutritional support run in parallel, adjusted as you evolve, with further testing where useful.

07

The outcome

Health and vitality that match your ambition: clearer thinking, a real sense of control over your diet and weight, and energy and emotional reserves you thought you had left behind in your twenties.
